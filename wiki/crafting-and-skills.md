# Crafting and skills

## Crafting
Open the crafting panel with C. It lists what you can make, grouped in tiers. The first tier uses bare hands and found materials. Each tool you make unlocks more recipes; a sharp stone edge, for example, does things your hands cannot. A better-quality tool also works a little faster. With the right tool you can chop logs from trees; a tree falls after a few logs and grows back in time.

How long a craft takes depends on what you make: bigger and more advanced items take longer. Skill and a better tool make it faster.

## Stations
Basic crafts (fire, stone tools, digging, cordage, clay preparation and the first hafted tools) can be made anywhere from the crafting panel. Other items need a crafting station. The stations are:

- **Crafting Table:** baskets, simple clothing and general items.
- **Potter's bench:** shaping pots and jars.
- **Spindle:** spinning wool and weaving cloth.
- **Leather drying rack:** working hides into leather.
- **Repair table:** mending worn tools.

Some stations need wood from a felled tree, so you will want an axe early. Stations are built from the build menu and only inside a claim you have rights to. You work standing next to one, and it uses materials from what you carry, from storage close to it, and from the claim's shared storage.

What you make goes into your bag. Things such as bricks, fired pots, storage jars, baskets and logs can be set down on the ground: drag one out of the bag (I) over the world, press its hotbar key, or take the "Place" prompt right after crafting it. A see-through outline follows the cursor and turns amber where it cannot go, such as water or steep ground. Click to set it down, turn it with T, Shift+T or the scroll wheel, and press Esc to put it back.

## Tools wear out
Spears, axes, knives, digging sticks and bow drills wear down as you use them. The item shows when it is worn or nearly spent. A spent tool stays in your bag but stops working until you mend it at a repair table. Mending costs far less than making a new tool, but each repair lowers its quality a little. Stone flakes, hand axes and stations do not wear out.

Some crafts are minigames, such as knapping stone and shaping clay. Your result depends on how well you play them. Press Esc to quit a minigame.

## Skills
Every craft trains a skill. Higher skill gives better results and faster work. Failed attempts still give some progress, at a reduced rate.

- **Survival basics** (fire, foraging, digging, simple tools, shelter, animal husbandry, endurance) are open to everyone with no limit. Cooking trains firecraft.
- **Specialities**, such as Potter, Builder, Leatherwork and Weaver, share a fixed total. You cannot max every speciality on one character, so choose which crafts to focus on. Players who specialise differently can make things for each other.
- **Titles** mark ranks within a skill. Partway up a skill you choose a focus within it.
- **Master rank** skips crafting minigames and gives a steady result. Hold Ctrl when starting a craft to play the minigame anyway, for a chance at a better result.

## The journal
Press J to open the journal. It records the materials you have found and the processes you know. Each process you know has a one-line reminder of how to do it again.

You can also learn a process from other players: by watching them do it, or by reading a note they have left on the ground (Use to read).
